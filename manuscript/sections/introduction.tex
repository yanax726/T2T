\section{Introduction}
Atopic dermatitis treatment must improve visible skin disease, itch and the burden of living with eczema. Treat-to-target care links repeated assessment of these outcomes to treatment review.\citep{williams2022home,debruin2021t2t} A useful severity estimate should express an individual patient's current condition and remain interpretable as treatment changes. This is especially relevant when rapid symptom relief precedes the later control of skin disease.

Existing frameworks differ in their timing and required depth of response. International recommendations link patient global assessment to disease-domain targets, whereas Canadian recommendations require both clinician-reported and patient-reported improvement. Chinese guidance includes earlier visits to assess itch relief.\citep{debruin2021t2t,yeung2023t2t,zhao2022chinat2t,zhao2026t2t} The AHEAD approach defines minimal disease activity through optimal targets selected with the patient; the International Eczema Council distinguishes low and very low disease activity from duration-defined control and remission.\citep{silverberg2024ahead,merola2026iec} These approaches preserve several clinical perspectives. They do not establish a blood-based measure that can recover the same clinical information.

Serum markers offer a link between clinical activity and inflammation. The p-EASI approach combines CCL17, IL-22 and soluble IL-2 receptor, with different coefficients before and during treatment.\citep{thijs2017} Subsequent studies reported Spearman correlations of 0.71 during cyclosporin treatment and 0.67 during dupilumab treatment.\citep{thijs2019,bakker2020} These studies established a practical precedent for converting blood measurements into Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) units. Applying such a calculation in another setting also requires agreement with individual scores, compatible assays and evaluation across the severity range.\citep{luijken2020,riley2024validation}

EASI combines the extent of affected skin with lesion intensity.\citep{hanifin2022,home_easi} A small-input equation combining affected body surface area (BSA) and serum measurements could offer a common calculation without separate rules for each drug or treatment phase. BSA supplies the clinical extent component; the serum panel supplies molecular information. Whether the latter improves accuracy must be tested directly. We use E-EASI, meaning estimated EASI, for this clinical--molecular research measure. It is intended to estimate recorded EASI at the visit; measured itch, quality of life and safety remain complementary assessments.

We investigated these questions in a Chinese cohort receiving dupilumab or two initial abrocitinib doses. We characterized clinical outcomes within the local target framework and followed all 23 measured serum markers, accounting for baseline differences and later treatment changes. We then developed a common BSA--serum equation and evaluated individual estimates, changes and response classification using patient-level validation. Matched input comparisons and transfer of molecular-only formulas to a published Chinese dataset tested what the molecular measurements contributed.
