\section{Results}
\subsection{Clinical framework and analysis populations}
The local framework progressed from evidence review and stakeholder input to two questionnaire rounds and steering-group review. It combined clinical-sign and patient-experience targets at W4, W12 and W24; W2 assessed itch alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure1}). Its timing and target definitions are compared with other frameworks in Supplementary Table~S13. E-EASI was evaluated as a parallel research measure and did not determine treatment decisions.

The clinical comparison included 163 patients: 65 started dupilumab, 56 abrocitinib 100 mg and 42 abrocitinib 200 mg. Median baseline EASI was 15.20, 11.70 and 19.35, respectively; age, sex and BMI also differed (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). Clinical treatment comparisons and linked-serum modelling used separate eligibility rules, detailed in Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{39}.

\subsection{Clinical control improved under the three initial treatment strategies}
Observed EASI, PP-NRS and adult DLQI decreased from W0 to W12 and W24; the component target rates describe the corresponding clinical control (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure2}). At W2, PP-NRS was at most 4 in 15/27 evaluable dupilumab recipients, 34/37 receiving initial abrocitinib 100 mg and 33/36 receiving initial abrocitinib 200 mg. The adjusted 100 mg--dupilumab difference was 41.7 percentage points (95\% CI, 15.1--69.4; Holm-adjusted $P=0.0095$). Adding baseline itch gave a similar difference of 41.4 points (13.0--66.8). W2 itch was unavailable in 38/65, 19/56 and 6/42 patients, respectively, leaving these associations sensitive to follow-up selection. Weighted worst-case bounds permitted reversal of the W2 contrast (Supplementary Methods).

Continuous itch comparisons also favoured both initial abrocitinib groups at W2. Adjusted PP-NRS was lower than with dupilumab by 2.14 points for 100 mg (95\% CI, 0.82--3.46) and 2.24 for 200 mg (0.83--3.65; both Holm-adjusted $P=0.0049$). Differences remained at W24: dupilumab-minus-abrocitinib contrasts were 1.62/1.60 PP-NRS points and 2.98/3.32 EASI points for 100/200 mg, respectively, with all four Holm-adjusted $P<0.015$. The estimated itch gaps were 0.52 and 0.64 points smaller at W24 than W2, but direct intervals for these changes included zero ($-1.98$ to 0.93 and $-2.31$ to 1.03 for later-minus-early differences). These data support an early itch association, without establishing convergence or equivalence of later efficacy. Complete estimates and intervals are supplied in the clinical contrast tables.

Recorded IGA distributions shifted towards lower grades (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure3}). At W24, the combined physician- and patient-reported target was reached by 26/36 (72.2\%), 22/24 (91.7\%) and 20/23 (87.0\%), respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure2}o). Counting all unknown outcomes as failures or successes gave initial-cohort bounds of 40.0--84.6\%, 39.3--96.4\% and 47.6--92.9\%. Later management included continuing and reducing treatment. Pathway source tables retain all initial patients and distinguish W12 response, subsequent reduction and unavailable management records. They describe management after response, without estimating a randomized dose-reduction effect.

Removing the IGA component from the reconstructed framework changed combined-target success to failure in 4/90, 6/112 and 0/83 jointly evaluable patients at W4/W12/W24, respectively (Supplementary Table~S10). This definition sensitivity preserved the W24 combined classifications; it did not validate the implemented IGA scale.

The lower unadjusted EASI in the 100 mg group did not persist as a clear dose-group difference after baseline adjustment. The adjusted 100-minus-200 mg difference was 0.62 points at W12 (95\% CI, $-2.08$ to 3.33) and 0.34 at W24 ($-1.10$ to 1.77). Weighting improved baseline balance, although endpoint-specific residual imbalance remained (Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{40}). Three W0 regimen entries conflicted with the initial-dose label and remain unresolved. Safety documentation through W24 was present for 27/65, 20/56 and 19/42 patients; these counts do not establish event incidence or treatment attribution (Supplementary Table~S3).

BSA, SCORAD and laboratory measures appear in Supplementary Figs.~\suppfig{3}--\suppfig{4}; all target contrasts appear in Supplementary Figs.~\suppfig{23}--\suppfig{26}. The all-available IgE model retained 258 measured values from 147 patients, compared with 30 patients with all three visits. Its W24-to-baseline ratios of $(1+\mathrm{IgE})$ were 0.38 (95\% CI, 0.23--0.61) for dupi, 0.70 (0.41--1.17) for abro 100 mg and 0.85 (0.51--1.43) for abro 200 mg (Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{21}; Supplementary Table~S17). The 100-versus-200 mg ratio of baseline-relative change was 0.81 (0.39--1.69; Holm-adjusted $P=1.00$). CAMS-only and centre-by-visit sensitivities likewise did not establish a dose difference. Thus the ordering of these curves does not show a superior IgE response with 100 mg. Earlier donor and Gaussian imputation results remained sensitive to missing-data assumptions for abro 200 mg (Supplementary Table~S8).

Huashan contributed 20/5/4 observed IgE values at W0/W12/W24, but none of its dupi follow-up values. Pooled trajectories therefore required a shared centre-pattern assumption; the CAMS-only curves supplied a directly supported follow-up sensitivity. Eosinophil modelling used 320 measurements from 138 patients. No W4 IgE or dupi W4 eosinophil value was measured. Hollow points in the fitted trajectories illustrate a smooth W0--W12 interpolation, not restored laboratory observations (Supplementary Figs.~\suppfig{4}b,d and \suppfig{21}). The fitted dupi W4 eosinophil level was 0.310 (conditional 95\% CI, 0.243--0.381); its interval does not account for an unobserved transient rise. The earlier 15-patient paired-median interpolation, a different population and statistic, remains in Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{46} as a sensitivity.
A post hoc application of the IEC EASI-and-itch thresholds classified 91/123 patients (74.0\%) as having low disease activity at W12 and 81/93 (87.1\%) at W24. Very low disease activity was present in 50/123 (40.7\%) and 54/93 (58.1\%), respectively. These proportions use patients with both scores observed at that visit; treatment-specific rates and full-group missing-outcome bounds are given in Supplementary Table~S16. They describe single-visit states, not sustained control or remission.

\subsection{Serum trajectories differed across initial treatment groups}
All 23 measured markers were retained. Among all available W0/W12 pairs, dupi recipients had lower CCL17, CCL18, CCL26, CCL11, IL-36$\beta$ and IFN-$\beta$ at W12 (BH q=0.0011--0.0325; Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{5}). Figure~\ref{fig:Figure4} instead follows only patients with all three visits available for each marker. In that subset, CCL17 and CCL26 met the W12 significance criterion in dupi; the other four did not. CCL17 remained lower at both follow-ups in dupi and abro 200 mg, as did IL2RA in both abrocitinib groups (all q$<0.05$ within the 138-comparison family). These unadjusted within-group findings do not test differences between drugs.

\subsection{One equation estimated follow-up EASI closely across visits}
The model dataset contained 316 complete-input visits from 160 patients: 152 at W0, 89 at W12 and 75 at W24. Development used 241 W0/W12 visits from 156 patients. The final equation combined BSA, CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5 with the same calculation before and during treatment. It required no treatment label, visit week or previous score. Observed-versus-estimated score distributions are shown in Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{44}.

The fitted equation multiplied affected area by a serum-based component. The two-decimal display was
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathrm{EASI}} \approx
\operatorname{clip}_{[0,72]}\Bigl\{\frac{\mathrm{BSA}}{100}
\bigl[&51.27+0.98\log_2(\mathrm{CCL17})\\
&+0.96\log_2(\mathrm{CCL26})
-0.83\log_2(\mathrm{IL\mbox{-}5})\bigr]\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:common}
\end{equation}
BSA is entered from 0 to 100 and concentrations use the development export's recorded pg/mL numerics, with the logarithm applied once. Here, clip bounds the result to 0--72, and log$_2$ is the base-two logarithm. E-EASI denotes this estimated EASI; it is a hybrid clinical--molecular measure, not a separately calibrated blood-only inflammation scale. The calculator retains full-precision parameters; the displayed approximation differed from final-equation predictions by less than 0.08 EASI points in this dataset. Threshold decisions use the precise calculator. Treatment, stage, time and previous scores are not inputs. Reported primary performance evaluates the nested selection procedure; it is not the apparent fit of this final equation.

MAE under nested patient-level validation was 3.19 at W0 (95\% CI, 2.75--3.70) and 1.30 at W12 (0.96--1.71); same-cohort W24 temporal application gave 1.19 (0.89--1.53). Spearman correlations were 0.918, 0.927 and 0.904; concordance correlations were 0.910, 0.900 and 0.869 (Table~\ref{tab:model}). The error distributions (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure5}d) showed that 41.8\%, 84.3\% and 82.0\% of predictions were within two EASI points. These describe individual errors, rather than agreement between group means. Mean signed errors at W12/W24 were +0.82/+0.84 points, with calibration slopes of 0.81 (95\% CI, 0.66--0.94) and 0.73 (0.59--0.82), indicating residual overestimation and scale miscalibration.

Removing eight W0 and two W12 reference-inconsistent visits from training, while retaining the original folds and evaluation outcomes, gave sensitivity MAEs of 3.19/1.15/1.06. This improved follow-up error but did not establish molecular added accuracy over the identically refitted BSA-area control. \subsection{Area and symptom inputs accounted for most predictive accuracy}
Matched input ablation used the same 151/89/75 observations at W0/W12/W24 for every comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure5}a--c). Adding BSA to the three serum markers reduced MAE from 7.67/7.48/8.56 to 3.20/1.29/1.19 points. Adding current PP-NRS further reduced follow-up error to 1.17 and 1.04 points; paired MAE differences were $-0.127$ (95\% CI, $-0.236$ to $-0.011$) and $-0.145$ ($-0.246$ to $-0.047$). The matched BSA-plus-PP-NRS controls gave 1.10 and 0.96 points, so this improvement was attributable to symptom information rather than an established serum advantage.

% INSERT: Figure5

Three markers did not lower MAE relative to CCL17 alone. With the area structure held constant, adding three markers to BSA reduced follow-up MAE by only 0.031 and 0.015 points, with both intervals spanning zero. In the separate adult DLQI subset, adding the serum panel to BSA, PP-NRS and DLQI reduced W24 MAE by 0.072 points (44 patients; 95\% CI, 0.021--0.124), while increasing baseline error. These exploratory input comparisons did not establish that all three markers were necessary or optimal (Supplementary Tables~S6--S7). The frozen equation was retained; the expanded-input models were not substituted for it.

Further molecular-only analyses tested nonlinear relationships, within-person changes during training and an auxiliary ordinal IGA target, while keeping deployment inputs limited to serum measurements. On the same core patients, nested selection among these candidates gave three-marker W0/W12/W24 MAEs of 7.50/7.21/7.80. A 19-marker analysis gave 7.41/7.26/8.49. These results did not approach the follow-up accuracy of E-EASI; the expanded panel did not provide a consistent improvement (Supplementary Table~S14). The primary equation was unchanged.

\subsection{Monitoring precision depended on the interval being assessed}
Estimated changes tracked the ordering of observed improvement, with different absolute errors across intervals (Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{45}). Change MAEs were 3.87 for W0--W12 (85 patients; 95\% CI, 3.08--4.70), 3.47 for W0--W24 (67 patients; 2.68--4.39) and 1.49 for W12--W24 (55 patients; 1.00--2.19). Corresponding Spearman correlations were 0.873, 0.940 and 0.900. The two intervals starting at baseline retained greater error despite their high correlations.

Using estimated scores at both visits to identify EASI75 (Fig.~\ref{fig:Figure5}e) gave sensitivities of 76.5\% at W12 and 86.1\% at W24, with conservative conditional 95\% bounds of 60.3--88.6\% and 72.2--94.8\%. Specificities were 98.6\% (86.0--100\%) and 96.2\% (65.7--100\%), based on 36 and 13 observed nonresponders. The broad bounds reflect the limited nonresponder samples; they combine repetition-specific binomial intervals, not independent copies of the patients. Sensitivity for EASI90 was lower, at 63.0\% and 52.9\% (Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{38}). These paired evaluations support examining response alongside the absolute reading, while preserving the distinction between monitoring agreement and clinical interchangeability.

\subsection{Future-response prediction remained exploratory}
Baseline CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5 gave a W12 itch-improvement AUROC of 0.630 in 102 patients. Adding them to baseline clinical information changed AUROC by 0.026 (95\% CI, $-$0.021 to 0.074). Training-selected compact panels had higher internal discrimination, but performance fell when entire assay-report sources were held out. In a separate 68-patient analysis, W12 concentrations of the original three markers did not improve prediction of W24 EASI75 over clinical information available through W12. These analyses concern future response rather than contemporaneous EASI estimation (Supplementary Tables~S18--S20).

\subsection{Molecular-only transfer did not establish an advantage over p-EASI}
In the published Chinese OMIX002850 dataset, an existing three-marker molecular-only equation was applied without refitting. Its W0/W16 MAEs were 11.51/6.92, compared with 11.27/3.89 for unchanged p-EASI, on the same 125/60 patients. The W16 MAE difference was 3.03 points (95\% CI, 1.65--4.38), despite a higher Spearman correlation for the three-marker equation (0.557 versus 0.364). The local matched analysis likewise did not establish molecular-only superiority (Supplementary Table~S15). OMIX002850 lacks BSA and therefore cannot validate E-EASI. This retrospective formula-transfer comparison was also limited by previous exploration of the dataset, unexcluded participant overlap and unbridged assay scales; it was not independent external validation.
