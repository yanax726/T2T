\section{Supplementary Methods}
\subsection{Study design and clinical records}
This analysis used longitudinal records from a Chinese observational treat-to-target project. The supplied protocol specified clinician-directed systemic treatment rather than random allocation. We retained the initial treatment group for longitudinal comparisons and separately extracted subsequent dose, interval, stopping, switching and restart information. The analysed records came from CAMS and Huashan. The protocol's planned centre list and planned sample size were not substituted for the observed cohort.

The clinical dose-comparison population included patients aged at least 12 years with a known initial regimen. Six of 170 source patients were below age 12 and one had an unresolved initial dose, leaving 163 patients (65/56/42 by initial treatment). Records with unresolved simultaneous initial-dose assignments were excluded from dose-specific comparisons but retained in the source audit. Adult DLQI analyses used age at least 18. Baseline Table 1 uses one observed W0 record per eligible patient, with no weighting or imputation. Continuous summaries use median and quartiles with linear interpolation, rank $1+(n-1)p$ (type 7; Excel PERCENTILE.INC); all available denominators are reported. Age and BMI are also presented as medians, rather than means and standard deviations. Repeated records were linked by the study patient identifier and nominal visit; a patient was never treated as a new independent person at each visit. The requested reporting visits were W0, W2, W4, W12 and W24 for PP-NRS; W0, W4, W12 and W24 for EASI, IGA, DLQI, BSA, SCORAD and eosinophils; and W0, W12 and W24 for IgE. Low-coverage intermediate visits were retained in the underlying data but were not inserted into these trajectories.

EASI was the recorded clinical reference outcome. Source discrepancies between regional sums and total EASI were audited rather than silently repaired. IGA grades were retained as recorded, including grade 5. Confirmation of the exact IGA version and PP-NRS recall window remains necessary. Nominal clinical/serum visit linkage does not by itself verify that the blood draw and examination occurred on the same calendar day; exact dates and a permissible matching window require source confirmation. Among the 152/89/75 model visits at W0/W12/W24, 8/2/0 had complete regional scores whose sum differed from total EASI by more than 0.15; the largest absolute gaps were 18.7/8.5/0.0 points, and 1/1/4 visits lacked a complete regional reference. Restricting evaluation to all four regions observed with total--sum difference at most 0.01 retained 140/86/71 visits and gave MAEs 3.17/1.32/1.11. This evaluation-only sensitivity uses unchanged saved predictions. A separate training-exclusion sensitivity removed the eight W0 and two W12 discrepant visits from fitting, retained missing-region visits, and repeated preprocessing and inner structure/penalty selection within the original patient folds. Evaluation patients and recorded outcomes were unchanged. It gave W0/W12/W24 MAEs of 3.19/1.15/1.06; matched BSA controls were refitted identically. Neither analysis adjudicates which clinical reference is correct (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:reference_sensitivity}). Details are in \texttt{model\_reference\_QC\_audit.csv}.

The supplied protocol is internally inconsistent on IGA: its eligibility text states 0--4, whereas Appendix 5 defines 0--5, with grade 5 labelled very severe. All 22 recorded grade-5 values occurred at baseline (9/2/11 by initial treatment) and were retained. A post hoc definition sensitivity removed the IGA alternative from the physician domain, leaving EASI50 at W4, EASI75 at W12 and EASI75 or EASI$\leq5$ at W24; patient-domain rules were unchanged. Both definitions were compared on the same jointly evaluable patients, and their own available-case denominators were also reported. Three-valued AND/OR logic preserved unknown components. An additional sensitivity excluded the 22 patients with baseline IGA5; this was a different population, not a correction of known errors. Complete paired transitions and patient-deduplicated changes are supplied in the IGA-definition source tables and Supplementary Table~S10. The protocol also specified 24-hour peak itch recall, without confirming its consistent implementation in the registry.

\subsection{Local framework and comparison with published targets}
The project presentation describes evidence review, stakeholder consultation, a questionnaire on assessment instruments, feedback and discussion, a second questionnaire on targets and timing, and steering-group review. The planned stakeholder sample comprised 30 physicians, 10 nurses and 10 patients; 49 returned questionnaires were reported. The planned agreement threshold was at least 75\%. These source descriptions do not provide individual ballots, round-specific denominators or item-level agreement. The flow diagram therefore represents the documented process, without assigning unverified voting results. Later month-9/12 targets in the source framework were not evaluated as observed outcomes in this 24-week cohort.

Supplementary Table~S13 compares the local framework with international 2021, Chinese 2022, Canadian 2023, AHEAD 2024, STRIVE AD and IEC 2026 recommendations. Original English publications were checked; the Chinese guidance thresholds were cross-checked against the guidance authors' 2026 clinical-trial analysis.\citep{debruin2021t2t,zhao2022chinat2t,zhao2026t2t,yeung2023t2t,silverberg2024ahead,gkini2026strive,merola2026iec} The comparison is descriptive and does not retrospectively redefine the study endpoints. AHEAD minimal disease activity depends on agreed optimal targets. Using its EASI branch, IEC low disease activity requires EASI $\leq7$ and PP-NRS $\leq4$, and very low disease activity requires EASI $\leq3$ and PP-NRS $\leq1$. IEC complete control and off-drug remission additionally require persistence for at least six and twelve months, respectively. These duration-defined states were not inferred from isolated visits. Recorded local IGA was not silently recoded as vIGA-AD.

\subsection{Clinical targets and treatment comparisons}
The week-2 target was PP-NRS $\leq4$. At week 4, the physician domain was EASI50 or an IGA decrease of at least two points, and the patient domain was PP-NRS $\leq4$ or adult DLQI $\leq10$. At week 12, the physician domain was EASI75 or IGA 0/1, and the patient domain was PP-NRS 0/1 or adult DLQI $\leq4$. At week 24, EASI $\leq5$ was an additional physician-domain criterion. The combined target required both domains. Because instrument identity and recall windows remain unresolved, these are exploratory targets based on the recorded scores. These are the investigators' operational definitions; they were not retrospectively relabelled as the newer IEC low-disease-activity or remission definitions.\citep{merola2026iec}

For an OR criterion, one observed positive component established success; a negative result required all components to be observed and negative. For an AND criterion, one observed negative component established failure; success required both positive components. Other cases remained unknown. Adult DLQI was an unknown component for ages 12--17, not a negative component and not removed from the OR rule. Thus adolescent itch success establishes the patient target; itch failure alone does not establish patient-target failure. The audit exhaustively verified the nine two-component truth states and reconciled every saved composite. Among 15 adolescents, the patient domain had 6/0/9 successes/failures/unknowns at W4, 4/0/11 at W12 and 4/0/11 at W24; the combined domain had 5/1/9, 4/5/6 and 4/3/8. An explicitly different PP-NRS-only adolescent rule would make five W12 and four W24 patient-domain unknowns determinate, including one W24 combined outcome. This is a sensitivity count, not a redefinition of the primary composite. Age-stratified counts are in \texttt{composite\_age\_evaluability\_audit.csv}. EASI response required an observed positive baseline. Individual thresholds were also analysed separately. Proportions used endpoint-specific observed denominators and Wilson 95\% confidence intervals. Overall three-group tests used conditional Monte Carlo Pearson tests with 49,999 draws, and pairwise comparisons used Fisher's exact test and risk-difference intervals. The three pairwise comparisons within an endpoint and visit formed a Holm family; exploratory FDR results were also retained. Overall tests were not included as an extra member of that pairwise family.

Baseline-adjusted binary target rates and risk differences used generalized overlap weights from a multinomial initial-treatment model with baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre. We used 1,000 patient bootstrap resamples, stratified by initial treatment and refitting the propensity model, for percentile intervals. Two-sided P values used normal Wald tests based on bootstrap standard errors; three-group tests used a two-degree-of-freedom Wald statistic. The three individual pairwise comparisons formed a Holm family. Weighted results supplement the observed numerator and denominator. The primary target analysis used bootstrap seed 20261017. A revision W2 sensitivity added baseline PP-NRS to the propensity model. One patient lacked baseline PP-NRS; both the original-covariate model and the added-itch model were fitted on the remaining 162 patients, with the original 163-patient analysis retained. The same standardized logistic model (ridge parameter $C=1$), 1,000 stratified patient resamples and seed 20261018 were used for this sensitivity. The revision files also contain a separate full-cohort run with seed 20261018. Its point estimates use the same analysis, but its Monte Carlo intervals and Wald P values are not the frozen primary results; borderline changes were not selected for the main claim. The added-itch 100 mg--dupilumab contrast was 41.4 percentage points (95\% CI, 13.0--66.8; three-contrast Holm $P=0.0104$); the 100-minus-200 mg contrast was 6.7 points ($-11.3$ to 26.2). The largest endpoint-specific absolute standardized difference was 0.53 even after adding itch, so residual imbalance remained. Baseline weights were retained when calculating available-case balance and ESS; no claim of balanced follow-up selection is made. The table below reports both unweighted and matching baseline-overlap-weighted worst/best bounds, assigning every unknown outcome failure/success while retaining baseline weights. These bound the initial cohort under extreme missing-outcome assignments; the reported weighted available-case rate represents patients with that endpoint observed. Endpoint-specific overlap-weight ESS is reported separately. Bounds are sensitivity limits, not confidence intervals.
\input{tables/headline_target_support} For generalized propensity scores $e_t(x)$, the weight is $h(x)/e_t(x)$, with $h(x)=1/\sum_t[1/e_t(x)]$. The baseline weighting function defines a common treatment-overlap population; an available-outcome analysis can depart from that population through selective observation. Covariate balance, propensity distributions and analysis-specific effective sample sizes are reported; weighting does not establish elimination of confounding.\citep{lili2019}

Continuous treatment comparisons used marginal regression with baseline outcome, treatment-by-visit terms, age, sex, BMI and centre. Generalized overlap weights addressed measured baseline differences in initial treatment selection. Patient-cluster robust covariance accounted for repeated outcomes conditional on the estimated baseline weights, without propagating their estimation uncertainty. A singular-value decomposition check prevented reporting non-estimable treatment-by-visit cells. Exploratory contrasts of treatment differences over time used the same fitted coefficients and full patient-cluster covariance, comparing W2 to W12/W24 for itch and W4 to W12/W24 for EASI. Holm correction covered the three treatment contrasts within each endpoint and interval; these tests did not infer a time interaction from different visit-specific significance levels. The earlier weighted IgE and eosinophil contrasts used log$(1+x)$; back-transformed estimates were not arithmetic means. The entirely unmeasured dupi W4 eosinophil cell was not estimated in that categorical-visit analysis. Revised all-available laboratory display models and explicitly assumed W4 interpolation are described below; they use a different estimand and replace the entire fitted display series. The earlier complete-three-visit IgE analysis remains a numerical sensitivity. No assumed W4 value completes an observed trajectory or enters a treatment test. Eosinophil paired changes use all observed pairs for each interval, with descriptive t-based 95\% intervals on the recorded scale; these intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted treatment-comparison tests. Supplementary Table~S9 reports laboratory coverage and these paired changes. Unweighted adjustment, group balance, observation patterns and supplementary dupilumab-versus-pooled-abrocitinib contrasts were retained. Pooling used the observed dose mixture and did not erase the two initial abrocitinib groups. The comparisons estimate adjusted observational associations, not randomized fixed-dose effects. Nominal eligibility based on the registry filename date placed all initial patients beyond W24, but this administrative sensitivity is not an approved database lock or verified visit window. Reasons for absent assessments were unavailable.

Treatment reductions were assigned from recorded regimen intervals, with assessment/decision ordering marked uncertain at shared visit boundaries. A later reduction was not moved to an earlier visit. W12 response and subsequent regimen changes defined descriptive maintenance pathways. Analyses used observed W24 EASI75, explicit missing-outcome bounds and fixed-patient trajectories where all three visits were observed. This approach distinguishes treatment initiation from the selected maintenance pathways, consistent with the need to specify how intercurrent treatment changes enter the question being estimated.\citep{iche9r1} It does not identify a counterfactual outcome under a different regimen. At W0, two source records labelled initial 100 mg explicitly listed a 200 mg starting regimen, and one labelled initial 200 mg listed 100 mg. These are conflicts between the source group and regimen fields, not demonstrated post-baseline switches. The group labels were retained, and \texttt{exposure\_W0\_source\_adjudication.csv} preserves their counts separately from unknown exposure. Clinical-record adjudication is still required.

\subsection{Serum measurements and molecular analyses}
The laboratory source reports identify serum samples analysed with Luminex and MSD platforms. Luminex used an R\&D Systems LXSAHM-13 panel and Luminex-200; the MSD reports identify K15067M-1/K15067M-2 kits and an SQ120 instrument. Reports describe 50-$\mu$L serum and twofold dilution. The service-batch sample count was not used as the study sample size. Assay lots, run identifiers and source links remain in the restricted provenance records.

The analysis used the numeric integration export rather than the vendor's filtered display matrix. Positive export numerics recorded in pg/mL were log$_2$ transformed once, unchanged; the analysis applied no additional twofold dilution correction. This reproduces the fitted scale rather than verifying how the laboratory corrected its export for dilution. Explicit censoring strings and nonpositive values were not converted to measured positive concentrations. The vendor's filtered output can replace values below range with zero and values above range with an upper bound; those conventions must not be interpreted as exact measured concentrations. A sensitivity analysis represented IL-5 values below twice the software calibration lower bound at that boundary and repeated the model workflow. The software boundary is not an independently validated clinical quantification limit. Cross-platform bridging, technical-repeat precision and lot effects require additional assay documentation.

All 23 measured markers were retained. Molecular changes were formed within each patient and visit pair before group summaries, avoiding subtraction of averages from different patient sets. We analysed W0--W12, W12--W24 and W0--W24 separately, retaining all available pairs for each marker and initial treatment. No measurement at the third visit was required. Mean log$_2$ changes were accompanied by pointwise t-based 95\% confidence intervals when at least five pairs were available. Smaller sets were descriptive. Two-sided paired mean-change sign-flip tests retained zero changes and ties. They enumerated all $2^n$ signs when $n\leq15$; otherwise they used 19,999 random signs (seed 20261002), counting exceedances at the observed absolute mean with a plus-one numerator and denominator. All-zero changes gave $P=1$. The sign-flip null assumes independent patients and sign-exchangeable paired changes (symmetry about zero); it is not a randomized-treatment test. Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment covered the full 23-marker, three-treatment, three-window family: 207 planned cells, of which 193 had estimable P values. Non-estimable cells remained missing and were not counted as tests. These within-group analyses did not adjust for clinical baseline covariates. The pointwise intervals were not multiplicity-adjusted and used a different inferential procedure from the sign-flip tests; excluding zero did not necessarily imply a BH-significant star. Between-treatment change models adjusted for starting concentration, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre, with HC3 covariance. Centre-specific and assay-batch sensitivity analyses were retained. Each between-treatment molecular correction family included all marker, window and pairwise contrasts within one centre scope and analysis variant; scopes and variants were not pooled. Molecular--clinical change correlations formed their own panel-wide family across markers, clinical outcomes, treatments and windows, and response-depth contrasts formed a separate family. The global molecular treatment-by-visit family had 23 planned marker tests, with 21 estimable. A source-table inventory records the defined families and finite-test counts. Spearman correlations related molecular changes to clinical changes within treatment groups; baseline regional correlations were assessed separately. Sample sizes, intervals and multiplicity families accompany each source table.

Regional analyses used source-consistent regional totals. Regional EASI contributions were divided by their theoretical maxima (7.2, 14.4, 21.6 and 28.8 for head/neck, upper limbs, trunk and lower limbs). Percentage improvement required a positive regional starting value; zero-start exclusions were counted. Because very low W12 values can produce extreme W12--W24 percentage changes, the corresponding figure instead shows change as a percentage of the theoretical regional maximum. Both definitions are retained in the tables. Regional treatment contrasts used unadjusted Welch comparisons and HC3 models adjusted for starting regional severity, baseline total EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre, with FDR over the 48 region-by-window-by-contrast tests in each analysis/outcome family. Whole-body PP-NRS change was not labelled as regional itch.

\subsection{Fixed-patient three-visit molecular display}
For each marker and initial treatment, the main molecular figure retained patients with positive recorded concentrations at W0, W12 and W24. Patients missing W24 were excluded even from its W12 comparison. This fixed-three-visit set was a subset of the all-available W0/W12 pairs; patient sets could differ between markers. Each patient's baseline log$_2$ concentration was subtracted from their follow-up value before averaging; baseline was zero by construction. Pointwise Student-t 95\% intervals and two-sided sign-flip tests required at least five patients. All signs were enumerated for $n\leq15$; larger sets used 49,999 random sign assignments with a plus-one correction. Tests with provisional q values between 0.04 and 0.06 were refined by exact enumeration for $n\leq24$, or 1,999,999 random assignments otherwise, before the same family was recomputed. This addressed simulation precision without changing the hypotheses or populations.

The Benjamini--Hochberg family comprised 138 planned comparisons (23 markers, three initial treatments, two follow-ups). The 14 non-estimable hypotheses contributed $P=1$ to the correction but had no displayed q value. Stars identify within-group changes with q$<0.05$; they do not test treatment differences. The all-available-pair family remained separate because it used different patient sets and three time windows. For dupi CCL18, the fixed-three-visit W12 comparison used 13 patients (q=0.147), versus 23 all-available pairs (q=0.0079). For CCL11, these counts were 24 (q=0.0977) and 36 (q=0.0276). All six markers significant at W12 in the all-pair analysis had negative mean changes in both populations. Restricting the population changed both the estimated changes and their uncertainty; a missing star did not establish absence of change. Censored and other unavailable assay states were counted separately rather than substituted into these distributions.

\subsection{Common EASI estimator and validation}
The initial discovery registry required at least 70\% baseline assay availability for model candidates, leaving 19 of 23 measured markers. Separate supervised searches ranked markers within training patients and compared panel sizes using inner validation; the final small EASI panel from that search contained CCL17 alone. In a subsequent exploratory revision, CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5 were fixed as a compact, biologically motivated candidate with relatively complete measurements. Supporting literature motivated evaluation of CCL17 and CCL26 in AD activity and of related type-2 markers; it did not validate this exact combination.\citep{Holm2021Immunoinflammatory,Kagami2003CCL26} The initial three-marker index standardized log$_2$ concentrations using training-reference means and standard deviations and averaged them equally. That relative molecular index was distinct from the later BSA--serum EASI equation and the historical six-marker inflammation score. In the present estimator, inner validation selected formula structure and penalty, not the identities of the three fixed markers. Matched CCL17-only, BSA-plus-CCL17 and leave-one-marker-out controls did not establish that this panel was optimal or that every component provided a stable accuracy gain (Tables S6--S7).

Eligibility required age at least 12, observed EASI and BSA, and observed positive CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5 concentrations. Ambiguous initial-dose labels did not exclude otherwise eligible model records, because treatment was not a required input. The three-marker panel arose from earlier exploratory work and was fixed for this revision. No clinical outcome was imputed. Model development used W0 and W12; W24 did not enter fitting or selection. Because earlier project analyses had already examined W24, its results are described as within-cohort temporal application rather than untouched external validation.

All observations from one patient remained in the same outer fold. Five-fold validation was repeated twice using the established patient partitions. Each training fold contained development visits only. Three-fold patient-level inner validation selected the ridge penalty from 0.1, 1, 10 and 100, minimizing mean per-patient absolute error. Each patient received equal total fitting weight. Feature centring and scaling used training data only. Three common structures were considered: an additive BSA-plus-three-marker model; the same model with quadratic BSA; and BSA multiplied by a linear molecular component without a free intercept. Structure and penalty selection were nested together for the primary performance estimate. No treatment, phase, time, previous score, age or BMI was required at deployment.

After full-development inner selection, the fitted common equation was
\begin{align*}
\widehat{\mathrm{EASI}}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,72]}\{\mathrm{BSA}\,[&0.512721579
 +0.009847080\log_2(\mathrm{CCL17})\\
 &+0.009646929\log_2(\mathrm{CCL26})
 -0.008270558\log_2(\mathrm{IL\mbox{-}5})]\}.
\end{align*}
BSA is entered as a percentage from 0 to 100, and concentrations must use the development export's recorded pg/mL numeric scale, with log$_2$ applied once. The implementation rejects missing, nonfinite or nonpositive molecular inputs and invalid BSA values. The area constraint returns zero when BSA is zero. It is a statistical constraint reflecting absent observed lesion extent, not proof of absent molecular inflammation. The released parameter file retains full precision. The main-text display multiplies the molecular coefficients by 100 and uses BSA/100, allowing two decimal places without dropping the smaller molecular terms. An explicit rounding audit gave nested-selection W0/W12/W24 MAEs of 3.194/1.298/1.193, compared with 3.194/1.297/1.192 at full precision. All primary results use full precision; the rounded equation is a reading aid. For the final fitted equation, maximum absolute arithmetic differences were 0.075550, 0.024807 and 0.018474 EASI points at W0/W12/W24; these are rounding differences, not clinical prediction errors. Response classifications at EASI50/75/90 were unchanged in the audit, but one of 150 week-24 fold-level evaluations crossed the absolute EASI 7 threshold, so threshold-based use should retain full precision. This single final fit has not received independent validation; primary metrics evaluate the nested development procedure that selected it.

Comparators used the same patients and folds: BSA alone, the three markers alone, the additive common model, and the earlier stage-interaction model. We evaluated MAE, RMSE, signed error, Spearman correlation, Lin's concordance correlation, $R^2$, calibration intercept and slope, severity-band agreement and proportions within one and two EASI points. Metrics were calculated within each repetition before averaging, not from averaged predictions. Patient bootstrap intervals used 2,000 resamples of held-out errors. The same sampled patient indices were used in every repetition and comparator; metrics were scored within each repetition before averaging. These intervals describe uncertainty conditional on the saved cross-validation predictions: neither nested selection nor fitting was repeated, and earlier adaptive marker exploration is not covered. Calibration intervals resampled patients in the regression of observed on predicted EASI. The primary nested-procedure minus area-only MAE differences were 0.12 (95\% CI, 0.04--0.20) at W12 and 0.17 (0.11--0.24) at W24. These paired estimates differ from the earlier fixed-structure comparison and do not establish molecular added accuracy. All comparator and fixed-structure results remain in the source tables. Descriptive agreement limits were mean error plus or minus 1.96 error standard deviations.\citep{bland2007}

Severity-band agreement used four categories: EASI $\leq7$, $>7$ to $\leq21$, $>21$ to $\leq50$ and $>50$. Error subgroup analyses instead combined the two highest categories, using three strata: EASI $\leq7$, $>7$ to $\leq21$ and $>21$. Binary sensitivity and specificity used EASI $>7$ and are not interchangeable with every original p-EASI classification definition. Leave-centre-out and leave-initial-treatment-group-out analyses removed all records from held-out patients. Changes were paired within patient, repetition and fold before averaging. Each of W0--W12, W0--W24 and W12--W24 was evaluated separately; the windows share patients. EASI50/75/90 classification required positive observed and estimated starting scores, with exclusions counted. Observed response used observed scores at both visits, and estimated response used estimated scores at both visits. Scatter plots and any integer confusion matrices use the first outer repetition; the primary response forest and principal metrics average repetition-specific values. They must not be interpreted as one common confusion matrix. EASI75 sensitivity used 49 and 54 observed responders at W12 and W24; specificity used 36 and 13 observed nonresponders, respectively, in both repetitions. For sensitivity, specificity and overall agreement, each repetition is reported with a Wilson 95\% binomial interval. For the repetition-average estimate, Clopper--Pearson intervals use a Bonferroni level of $1-0.05/R$ for each of $R$ repetitions, and their lower and upper limits are averaged separately. Within each endpoint, window and metric, these give conservative simultaneous bounds across repetitions under a conditional binomial working model without assuming independence between repetitions. They do not establish exact coverage for the full cross-validation procedure, whose shared fitted models and development uncertainty are not resampled. Patients are not multiplied by the number of repetitions. Repetition-level counts and intervals are in Supplementary Table~S11; average bounds are in \texttt{primary\_EASI75\_uncertainty.csv}. Earlier percentile-bootstrap limits are retained as explicitly labelled diagnostic columns, not the primary classification bounds. The empirical cumulative absolute-error curve is the average of the two repetition-specific proportions. Pointwise bootstrap intervals carry all saved predictions from each sampled patient together and do not refit the development procedure. Applying the published formulas used the published rounded coefficients without local refitting, with local IL2RA as a nominal sIL-2R proxy and identical eligible patients for both methods. Original p-EASI outputs were initially left unbounded to preserve the published arithmetic, whereas local estimates were clipped to 0--72; matched clipping of the original outputs was examined separately. Patient-paired bootstrap intervals were calculated for differences between the current and published-formula metrics, retaining both repetitions for each sampled patient. These intervals characterize the local comparison; they do not remove the difference in inputs or establish assay equivalence.\citep{thijs2017,thijs2019,bakker2020}

\subsection{Independent auxiliary target models}
PP-NRS and adult DLQI were fitted separately with original-scale or log$(1+x)$ ridge regression, using patient-level inner validation to select the link and penalty. Predictions were bounded to 0--10 and 0--30, respectively. Recorded IGA used a penalized proportional-odds model over the six observed grades, with ordered thresholds and six category probabilities. Normalized ranked probability score was the primary ordinal loss. All three targets used the same four-input panel for a practical shared measurement set, with BSA-only, molecular-only and training-only constant comparators. Neither EASI nor the target's current observed score was an input. Development and temporal application followed the patient-level W0/W12 and W24 separation used above. This limited shared-panel study does not claim to select the optimal molecular set independently for each target. Details and full estimates are in the supplement.

\subsection{Reproducibility and reporting}
The model analysis used Python 3.9.6, NumPy 2.0.2, pandas 2.3.3, SciPy 1.13.1 and scikit-learn 1.6.1. Exact dependency versions, analysis specifications, input/output hashes and execution logs accompany the code. Clinical and prediction reporting follow the relevant STROBE and TRIPOD+AI domains; applicability and risk-of-bias issues are recorded explicitly.\citep{vonelm2007strobe,collins2024tripod,moons2025probast} No non-significant difference, high correlation or overlapping mean trajectory was taken as proof of individual interchangeability.

\subsection*{Matched input ablation}
The original core population contained 316 visits from 160 patients; current PP-NRS was available for 315 visits from the same 160 patients. Adult current-itch and DLQI comparisons contained 207 visits from 111 patients. Models within each population used identical observations and the original patient outer folds. Development comprised W0/W12 only: 241 visits from 156 patients in the core, 240 visits from 156 in the itch comparison, and 163 visits from 107 in the adult comparison. Five outer folds with two repetitions and three inner patient folds selected ridge penalty and, for BSA-containing candidates, linear, BSA-quadratic or BSA-times-other-input structures. Serum-only and itch-plus-serum models used linear ridge. Standardization was fitted inside each training partition and each patient's total fitting weight was equal. Predictions were bounded to 0--72. No clinical outcome or deployment input was imputed.

CCL17-only, BSA-plus-CCL17 and fixed-area leave-one-marker-out comparisons tested panel dependence. Coefficients were summarized only within the same structure; different parameterizations were not averaged. All metrics were calculated within each repetition before averaging. Paired differences resampled the same patients across models and repetitions in 2,000 draws, conditional on saved predictions. These post-result exploratory comparisons did not replace the frozen formula and did not correct for the earlier panel selection. Complete metrics, paired changes, coefficients and selection frequencies accompany Tables S6--S7.

\subsection*{IgE observation patterns and multiple imputation}
The revised available-data display used every recorded IgE or eosinophil response at primary visits. On log$(1+x)$, a maximum-likelihood random-intercept model included initial-treatment-by-categorical-visit cells, log$(1+\mathrm{baseline\ EASI})$, age, sex, BMI and centre where variable. Baseline laboratory values entered as repeated outcomes with free treatment-specific means, rather than being required covariates in a follow-up ANCOVA. Patients with a single observed visit remained eligible. This contributed 147 patients and 258 IgE measurements across both centres, or 125 patients and 229 measurements within CAMS. Fixed-effect design rows were averaged over all eligible baseline patients in each scope, followed by inverse transformation; resulting values were shifted geometric means. Within-treatment ratios subtracted that group's fitted baseline on the log scale and exponentiated the contrast. Confidence intervals used the full fixed-effect covariance, including shared endpoint covariance. Six pairwise W12/W24 contrasts per outcome and analysis received Holm correction. A centre-by-visit sensitivity relaxed the time-constant centre difference, but neither pooled specification could directly identify an absent Huashan dupi follow-up cell. These models assume missingness is explainable by included observed information. They do not resolve nonignorable missingness or identify randomized drug effects. Supplementary Table~S17 gives IgE coverage and ratios; the earlier paired and imputed analyses below remain available as distinct sensitivities.

All IgE W4 cells and the dupi eosinophil W4 cell were entirely unmeasured. Optional display estimates used $m_4=(2/3)m_0+(1/3)m_{12}$ for fitted log$(1+x)$ group levels. If $v_w$ denotes the averaged fixed-effect design row, the conditional variance was $v_4^T\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(\widehat\beta)v_4$, where $v_4=(2/3)v_0+(1/3)v_{12}$. Inverse transformation produced the hollow point and interval. These estimates were never inserted into patient records, model fitting or treatment tests. Earlier-change and delayed-change time shapes remained numerical sensitivities. In particular, published dupilumab data show that early transient blood eosinophil rises can occur; interpolation does not exclude such a peak.\citep{wechsler2022eosinophils}

IgE was observed at W0, W12 and W24, with no W4 measurement. All available two-visit pairs were analysed without requiring the third visit. Paired t intervals used log(1+IgE), so exponentiated mean differences are geometric mean ratios of IgE+1, not arithmetic mean ratios. An available-data maximum-likelihood random-intercept model included treatment-by-visit, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre where variable, with unconstrained treatment-specific baseline means. CAMS (130 patients) supplied observed follow-up in every initial treatment; Huashan supplied no dupi IgE at W12 or W24. Full-cohort imputation of 163 patients therefore involved unsupported treatment--centre follow-up cells and was retained only as an additional sensitivity. Five CAMS patients, and 16 across both centres, had no IgE at any of the three visits; their imputations relied entirely on clinical covariates and relationships learned from other patients.

Fully conditional imputation used one wide record per patient. Four chains each underwent 200 burn-in cycles and retained 100 draws separated by ten cycles. Incomplete variables were the three IgE values and W12/W24 EASI; complete predictors were initial treatment, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre where variable. Future observed measurements served as auxiliaries for retrospective reconstruction, not prospective prediction. EASI auxiliary imputations were used only inside the imputation model, never as validation outcomes. Type-1 predictive mean matching used five closest observed same-treatment/same-visit donors on log(1+IgE). The alternative drew coefficients, residual variance and positive IgE values from Bayesian Gaussian regression on log(IgE), without an invented upper ceiling. Both assumed common conditional slopes across treatment groups with separate intercepts. Observed values were unchanged.

Rubin variance and Barnard--Rubin small-sample degrees of freedom pooled group means, changes and baseline-adjusted contrasts. The latter used baseline log(1+IgE), treatment-by-visit, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre, with fixed previously estimated baseline overlap weights and patient-cluster covariance. They condition on those weights and do not include propensity-estimation uncertainty. Initial groups were not reassigned after treatment changes. Illustrative MNAR scenarios multiplied only missing follow-up IgE+1 by one-half or two, retaining a zero lower bound for IgE. The repeated value 5000 was not assumed to be a verified censoring limit. Mixing, effective draws, missing-information fractions, Monte Carlo errors, donor support and predictive tails were checked. The maximum MAR change diagnostic was $\widehat R=1.013$, with Monte Carlo error at most 5.6\% of the inferential standard error. In CAMS abro 200 mg at W24, 13 donors supported 28 missing values; 25.8\% of PMM recipient fitted means lay outside the donors' fitted-mean range, averaged across imputations. Gaussian draws could extrapolate, but 7.6\% of imputed W24 values in that group exceeded the recorded maximum of 5000. Both methods were retained rather than selected by the direction of change; computational convergence did not remove this sensitivity.\citep{sterne2009mi,wijesuriya2025longitudinal}

\subsection*{Eosinophil interpolation sensitivity}
No dupi patient had an observed W4 eosinophil value. Within the 15 patients with W0 and W12 measurements, the estimated value was $\exp\{(2/3)\log(1+\mathrm{EOS}_0)+(1/3)\log(1+\mathrm{EOS}_{12})\}-1$. The group median and a conditional 95\% percentile interval used 2,000 patient bootstrap samples. A seven-patient subset observed at W0/W12/W24 was analysed separately; its estimated median was 0.396 (0.183--0.455), illustrating the effect of changing the population. Neither subset was spliced into an all-patient observed trajectory without identifying the denominator.

Sensitivity scenarios used raw-scale linear, early logarithmic-time and delayed quadratic-time interpolation. All assumed a smooth path and therefore did not encompass a transient rise. Masking observed W4 values in abro 100/200 mg supplied limited back-checks: log-scale interpolation MAEs were 0.162/0.222 recorded units in 22/11 patients. These checks were not validation of the missing dupi W4 measurements. No estimated EOS value entered target-rate calculations, treatment-effect inference or clinical prediction validation (Supplementary Fig.~\suppfig{46}).

\subsection*{Exploratory molecular-only model extension}
This extension used the original patient partitions and the core 316 visits from 160 patients (W0/W12/W24: 152/89/75). Development comprised 241 W0/W12 visits from 156 patients. Two repeats of five outer patient folds and three inner patient folds separated fitting, selection and evaluation. W24 did not enter fitting or tuning. Because this cohort had already been used extensively for model exploration, the comparison was exploratory and did not reset the validation history.

The compact panel contained CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5. The expanded panel contained the 19 markers already designated eligible for model discovery; CCL18, IL-13, IL-22 and IL-31 remained in descriptive analyses but were not promoted into the deployment panel because their earlier measurement limitations remained. Missing molecular values were replaced by the training-fold median and standardized using training-fold parameters. No clinical outcome was imputed and no missingness indicator was used. The models took only current molecular measurements at prediction, without BSA, clinical history, symptoms, treatment, time, centre or demographic inputs. Patient identity aligned folds and training pairs; recorded IGA served only as an auxiliary training outcome.

Thirty-four fixed candidates per panel comprised four weighted ridge models, 24 nonlinear radial-basis candidates and six auxiliary-target reduced-rank candidates. Ridge penalties were 0.1, 1, 10 and 100, with an unpenalized intercept and equal total fitting weight per patient. The nonlinear family used a 32-component Nystr\"om approximation, two kernel widths, three ridge penalties and paired-change weights of 0, 0.3, 1 and 3. Its loss added a penalty on disagreement between observed W0--W12 EASI change and the difference between the corresponding model estimates. A zero weight allowed nonlinear learning without the paired constraint. This training loss did not require a baseline sample at deployment.

The auxiliary-target family jointly fitted standardized EASI and five ordered indicators, IGA$\geq1$ through IGA$\geq5$, with the IGA block scaled to comparable total weight. A rank-one or rank-two projection of training fitted responses was then used; only the EASI output was retained for prediction. The procedure did not assume that IGA was continuous or validate its recorded scale. Inner-fold mean per-patient absolute EASI error selected the candidate, and predictions were bounded to 0--72. Family-specific and overall-selection results were both preserved (Supplementary Table~S14).

A separate expanded-data sensitivity removed the BSA and three-marker-completeness restrictions, requiring observed EASI, age at least 12, a linked blood sample and at least eight observed markers from the 19-marker panel. It retained 327 visits from 161 patients (155/94/78), with existing patients retaining their original folds. Development used 249 W0/W12 visits from 157 patients. Its 19-marker selection MAEs were 7.51/7.44/8.23; these were not directly substituted for the matched-population comparison. Intervals used 2,000 patient resamples of saved held-out predictions, with identical patients resampled across comparators and repetitions. These intervals condition on fitted predictions and do not include the full history of model adaptation. The primary E-EASI formula and parameters were unchanged.

\subsection*{Retrospective molecular formula-transfer benchmark}
Existing molecular-only equations fitted to local W0/W12 data were applied unchanged to OMIX002850; these were separate serum-only ablation models, not E-EASI with BSA deleted. Eligible observations required measured EASI between 0 and 72 and positive finite CCL17, CCL26, IL-5, IL-22 and the recorded soluble IL-2 receptor proxy. Healthy controls were excluded, and no missing input or outcome was imputed. The matched public-data analysis included 125 baseline and 60 W16 observations.\citep{wu2023}

The primary p-EASI comparator used the 2017 full-precision pretreatment/during-treatment coefficients; published rounded coefficients were a sensitivity. All methods were bounded to 0--72 identically for the primary comparison, with unbounded outputs retained separately. MAE, RMSE, signed error, Spearman correlation, concordance, calibration and error-tolerance proportions were calculated within visit. Changes were formed only after pairing the same 60 patients. Patient bootstrap intervals and paired MAE differences used 2,000 identical resamples across methods (seed 20261101). No model was fitted, tuned or recalibrated against the public-data EASI values in this benchmark.

A separate local comparison required all inputs for the molecular formulas and BSA, retaining 60/12/13 visits at W0/W12/W24. Saved patient-held-out predictions were evaluated within each repetition before averaging; paired patient resamples were shared across methods. Low IL-22 coverage restricted this matched subset. These were exploratory comparisons, without a confirmatory multiplicity claim (Supplementary Table~S15).

OMIX002850 had been examined in earlier project work, possible overlap with Huashan participants remained unresolved, and nominal concentration units did not establish assay equivalence. BSA was absent. Consequently the analysis was a retrospective formula-transfer benchmark, not independent external validation of E-EASI. Published trials and supplement tables were separately checked for compatible inputs; group summaries or relative proteomic signals were not substituted for missing individual absolute concentrations.

\subsection{Post hoc comparison with IEC low-activity thresholds}
The EASI branch of the IEC disease-control definitions was applied at W0, W4, W12 and W24: low disease activity required EASI $\leq$7 and PP-NRS $\leq$4; very low disease activity required EASI $\leq$3 and PP-NRS $\leq$1.\citep{merola2026iec} Primary proportions required both scores to be observed at the same nominal visit and used Wilson 95\% confidence intervals. Full-population bounds retained known failures when either observed component failed, and assigned unresolved states to failure or success. These descriptive, post hoc analyses neither imputed scores nor tested additional treatment contrasts. They do not reconstruct patient-selected AHEAD priorities or establish continuous on-treatment control or off-treatment remission.

\subsection{Exploratory baseline prediction of W12 itch improvement}
We tested whether baseline CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5 predicted a measured PP-NRS decrease of at least four points at W12. Eligibility required observed baseline PP-NRS $\geq$4 and observed W12 PP-NRS, using the current clinical population aged at least 12 with unambiguous initial treatment. The six younger participants retained in the historical analysis were excluded. Of 112 clinically evaluable patients, seven had no baseline assay and three lacked a usable CCL26 result, leaving 102 with all three markers. This primary population contained 88 responders and 14 nonresponders. A predictor-imputation sensitivity included 105 patients with at least one observed panel marker, comprising 89 responders and 16 nonresponders.

Three fixed L2-penalized logistic models used baseline log$_2$ concentrations alone, baseline clinical inputs alone, or the clinical inputs plus the three markers. Clinical inputs were age, sex, BMI, centre, initial treatment, EASI, PP-NRS, IGA, BSA and adult DLQI. Adolescent DLQI was masked. Within each training fold, numeric predictors underwent median imputation and standardization; categorical predictors underwent most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding. No response value was imputed. These logistic response models were separate from the frozen E-EASI equation, which estimates contemporaneous EASI.

The three models shared five stratified patient folds repeated five times, with three-fold inner validation selecting $C\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ by log-loss. All predictors came from baseline, and each patient appeared once per validation repetition. Primary AUROC, AUPRC, Brier and log-loss estimates averaged metrics calculated separately within each outer repetition. Metrics calculated after averaging each patient's repeated held-out probabilities were reported separately in the source tables for comparison with the historical evaluation. Paired intervals used 2,000 patient bootstrap samples, retaining all repetitions and model predictions from each sampled patient. These conditional intervals did not repeat fitting or account for the earlier exploration of this cohort. Calibration and training-fold event-rate predictions were retained as additional checks.

Serum-only AUROC was 0.630 (95\% CI, 0.513--0.736); clinical and combined AUROCs were 0.697 and 0.723, respectively (Supplementary Table~S18). Adding the markers changed AUROC by 0.026 (95\% CI, $-$0.021 to 0.074), with negligible Brier change ($-$0.00004). Serum-only AUROC was 0.599 (0.484--0.707) in the 105-patient sensitivity analysis. Molecular discrimination was modest and sensitive to the population; an incremental benefit over clinical inputs was not established. The analysis remained exploratory because the panel and outcome followed earlier cohort exploration, only 14 primary nonresponders were available, and no external validation was performed.

\subsection{Exploratory panel size and source-file sensitivity}
The same 102 and 105 patients, response labels and patient partitions were used to compare the original fixed panel with training-selected panels of 3, 4, 5, 6 or all 19 discovery-eligible markers. Candidate eligibility was unchanged: CCL18, IL-13, IL-22 and IL-31 were not introduced into selection. Median imputation, standardization and univariate ANOVA ranking were refitted inside each training pipeline. Each fixed size selected the ridge penalty by inner log-loss using the same grid as above. Serum-only and clinical-plus-serum procedures were evaluated separately; a procedure's AUROC was not assigned to one marker list chosen from the full dataset. Complete metrics and selected-input observation coverage accompany Supplementary Table~S19.

A further compact procedure selected size using inner validation only. After choosing the best penalty at each size, the lowest mean log-loss supplied a tolerance of one sample standard deviation across the three inner folds divided by $\sqrt{3}$. The smallest size from 3--6 within that tolerance was retained. This one-standard-error rule favoured simplicity and was not an equivalence or noninferiority test. All paired patient-bootstrap intervals conditioned on saved predictions; the panel-size comparisons had no simultaneous confirmatory error control.

On the 102-patient population, training-selected three- and four-marker clinical-plus-serum AUROCs were 0.800 (95\% CI, 0.680--0.900) and 0.808 (0.690--0.904). The fourth-marker difference was 0.008 ($-$0.007 to 0.025), without an established gain. The compact clinical-plus-serum procedure selected three markers in all 25 overlapping training folds, but produced eight different marker sets. Its AUROC difference from the clinical reference was 0.103 ($-$0.038 to 0.238), which did not establish added discrimination. Selection frequencies were not independent biological confirmations and did not define a validated fixed panel.

To assess dependence on data source, each of four original assay-report files was held out in turn. All preprocessing, size selection and penalty selection were repeated on the other files; source identity was not a predictor. Source files differed in centre, treatment and response prevalence and were not confirmed physical assay batches. One contained responders only, so its within-file AUROC was undefined; all its patients remained in pooled discrimination and probability-error calculations. Conditional intervals resampled patients within each file, preserving the four-source composition. In the 102-patient population, serum-only compact AUROC was 0.512 and clinical-plus-serum AUROC was 0.617, compared with 0.688 and 0.800 under patient-held-out validation. The corresponding 105-patient source-file-holdout AUROCs were 0.605 and 0.711. These results show source-and-case-mix sensitivity, without identifying a causal technical batch effect or establishing external transportability.

\subsection{W12 molecular concentrations and W24 EASI75}
A separate current-population analysis predicted W24 EASI75 from W12 information. Eligibility required age at least 12, unambiguous initial treatment, an observed positive pretreatment EASI, an observed W24 EASI and a W12 visit. W24 EASI75 was defined as measured W24 EASI no greater than one-quarter of pretreatment EASI, irrespective of whether the target had been reached at W12. Of 93 clinically evaluable patients, 68 had usable W12 CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5, including 59 responders and nine nonresponders. A 72-patient sensitivity required at least one available marker and included 63 responders and nine nonresponders. This used W12 concentrations, not the W0--W12 molecular changes used in the historical analysis.

Three fixed logistic models used W12 markers alone, clinical information through W12, or their combination. Clinical predictors were pretreatment EASI, age, sex, BMI, centre, initial treatment and W12 EASI, PP-NRS, IGA, BSA and adult DLQI. Adolescent DLQI was masked. No W24 predictor or imputed response entered fitting. Preprocessing, the $C$ grid, inner log-loss selection, five outer folds repeated five times and 2,000 paired patient bootstrap samples followed the baseline analysis above. Results were scored per repetition before averaging, with averaged-probability metrics retained separately. The limited nonresponder sample and previous cohort exploration precluded a confirmatory claim. Full results are in Supplementary Table~S20.

W12 marker-only AUROC was 0.406 (95\% CI, 0.267--0.544). Adding the markers to clinical information changed AUROC by $-$0.029 ($-$0.083 to 0.012) and Brier score by +0.00096 ($-$0.00355 to 0.00524). The 72-patient sensitivity likewise did not establish molecular added prediction. These results did not support a W12 three-marker prognostic tool for W24 EASI75.
