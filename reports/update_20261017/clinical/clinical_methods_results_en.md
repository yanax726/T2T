# Clinical and molecular analysis text

## Statistical methods

The clinical analysis included participants aged 12 years or older whose initial treatment could be assigned unambiguously. Analyses of DLQI were restricted to adults aged 18 years or older. Patients remained in their initial treatment group after dose reduction, discontinuation or switching. Actual regimens were summarised separately from initial treatment. Repeated records from one patient were not treated as independent participants.

Continuous measurements were summarised by the median and interquartile range. Clinical trajectories used the visits specified by the clinical team: weeks 0, 2, 4, 12 and 24 for PP-NRS; weeks 0, 4, 12 and 24 for EASI, BSA, DLQI, SCORAD and eosinophil count; and weeks 0, 12 and 24 for IgE. IGA was reported as its recorded category distribution and as binary treatment targets. Week 48 results were exploratory. Missing clinical measurements were not replaced with inferred scores.

The week-2 target was PP-NRS ≤4. At week 4, the physician target was EASI50 or an IGA improvement of at least two categories; the patient target was PP-NRS ≤4 or adult DLQI ≤10. At week 12, the physician target was EASI75 or IGA 0/1, and the patient target was PP-NRS 0/1 or adult DLQI ≤4. At week 24, EASI ≤5 was an additional physician criterion. Overall achievement required both the physician and patient domains. A domain was positive when any observed component met its criterion; it was negative only when all required components were observed and negative. Overall achievement was negative when either domain was known to be negative and otherwise remained indeterminate when necessary measurements were missing.

Observed target proportions were reported with their evaluable denominators and Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Unadjusted three-group comparisons used a conditional Monte Carlo Pearson test with 49,999 allocations. Pairwise comparisons used two-sided Fisher exact tests and Newcombe confidence intervals for risk differences. Holm adjustment was applied to the three pairwise comparisons within each endpoint and visit. Combined abrocitinib comparisons were supplemental and retained the contribution of the two initial dose groups.

Baseline treatment selection was addressed using generalised overlap weights derived from a multinomial logistic model containing baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre. Covariates were standardised before fitting; the logistic model used L2 regularisation with C=1. Weighted target rates used observed outcomes. Their confidence intervals were obtained from 1,000 patient bootstrap samples, resampled within initial treatment group; the propensity model was re-estimated in each sample. Pairwise P values used the bootstrap standard error with a normal approximation, and the three-group test used a two-degree-of-freedom Wald statistic. These P values and percentile confidence intervals were not treated as exact inverse tests. Missing-outcome bounds were also reported without assigning missing patients a true response status.

Adjusted continuous trajectories were estimated by weighted marginal least-squares models with the baseline value of the outcome, treatment, categorical visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, age, sex, BMI and centre. IgE and eosinophil counts were analysed after log(1+x) transformation. Standardised means used a common observed covariate distribution. Confidence intervals and contrasts used patient-cluster robust covariance estimates and t distributions with the number of patient clusters minus one degree of freedom. Singular-value decomposition was used to identify unsupported contrasts. An unweighted outcome-adjusted analysis was retained as a sensitivity analysis. Continuous-model intervals were conditional on the estimated overlap weights; they did not incorporate additional propensity-model uncertainty.

All 23 serum markers were retained in the descriptive inventory. Analyses used measured positive concentrations on the log2 scale; censored or unavailable concentrations were not imputed in these analyses. Changes were calculated within the same patient for weeks 0–12, 12–24 and 0–24. Mean paired changes were accompanied by t-based 95% confidence intervals and two-sided sign-flip P values; inferential estimates were withheld when fewer than five pairs were available. The sign-flip test assumes symmetry of the paired differences under the null. A complementary three-visit display used the same patients at all three visits for each marker, with each patient's baseline subtracted before averaging.

Between-treatment molecular contrasts used paired-change regression adjusted for the starting molecular concentration, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre, with HC3 standard errors. Assay-file adjustment was examined in a separate sensitivity analysis. Aliased nuisance coefficients were permitted only when the requested treatment contrast was demonstrably estimable. Analyses required at least five complete pairs in each treatment group and at least eight residual degrees of freedom; excessive leverage and unsupported contrasts were flagged. A repeated-measure marginal model tested the overall treatment-by-time interaction for each marker, with patient-cluster robust inference.

Spearman correlations related paired molecular changes to changes in EASI, PP-NRS, adult DLQI, IGA and BSA, separately by initial treatment. Correlations required at least eight pairs and nonconstant measurements; confidence intervals used patient bootstrap samples. Baseline associations between serum markers and regional EASI were pooled across initial treatment groups and restricted to records with reliable regional sums. These analyses describe serum–regional associations, not local tissue concentrations. False discovery rates were controlled separately within the predefined molecular analysis families. Non-estimable tests retained missing P and q values. All reported confidence intervals were nominal, not multiplicity-adjusted.

Post-week-12 dose-reduction paths were descriptive. Later reduction was not encoded as a baseline exposure or a randomised treatment decision. Continued EASI75 at week 24 was distinguished from continuously maintained control, which cannot be established from sparse visits. Safety analyses summarised documentation and preserved source descriptions for adjudication; blank records were not interpreted as absence of events.

## Selected results for integration with the manuscript

The analysis included 163 participants: 65 initially treated with dupilumab, 56 with abrocitinib 100 mg and 42 with abrocitinib 200 mg. Baseline median EASI was 15.20, 11.70 and 19.35, respectively. At week 2, the PP-NRS ≤4 rate in the 100 mg group was 34/37 (91.9%). The previously reported 35/38 included one participant with conflicting initial-dose records.

At week 24, observed overall target achievement was 26/36 (72.2%) with dupilumab, 22/24 (91.7%) with abrocitinib 100 mg and 20/23 (87.0%) with abrocitinib 200 mg. The weighted risk difference between the two abrocitinib groups was −1.5 percentage points (100 minus 200 mg; 95% CI, −28.7 to 23.9). The adjusted EASI difference between these groups was 0.34 points (95% CI, −1.10 to 1.77). These findings did not establish superiority of the lower initial dose.

Differences between dupilumab and abrocitinib were observed for several week-24 outcomes. However, baseline imbalance was reduced rather than eliminated, and the subset with observable week-24 outcomes was less well balanced than the full baseline cohort. Only 83 participants had sufficient information for the week-24 overall target. Treatment comparisons therefore describe associations with initial management strategies among observed patients, with substantial uncertainty from missing outcomes and subsequent treatment changes.

The apparently increasing IgE median in the 200 mg group reflected changing cross-sectional samples: 37, 22 and 14 measurements were available at weeks 0, 12 and 24. Among the 13 participants with paired baseline and week-24 measurements, median change was zero and mean change was −398.7 recorded units. The cross-sectional increase should not be described as a general within-patient increase.
