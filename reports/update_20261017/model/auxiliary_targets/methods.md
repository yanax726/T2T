# Separate fitting of the three auxiliary clinical targets

PP-NRS, adult DLQI and recorded IGA grades were fitted independently using the same restricted input panel: current BSA, log2 CCL17, log2 CCL26 and log2 IL-5. Each target has its own fitted coefficients and regularization choice. No current clinical outcome, EASI, treatment, visit week, phase, BMI or previous score was an input. The panel was fixed from the prior EASI work for a practical common measurement set; this is not a claim that the panel is optimal for each other target.

The current clinical table was joined to molecular measurements at nominal weeks 0, 12 and 24 by participant identifier and visit. Complete observed target and four-input records were analyzed. No clinical outcome or input was imputed. PP-NRS and recorded IGA analyses required age >=12; DLQI required age >=18, replacing the historical >16 scope. Counts at baseline/week 12/week 24 were 151/89/75 for PP-NRS, 105/58/44 for adult DLQI and 152/89/75 for recorded IGA. The flow table explicitly refers to assay-linked records and distinguishes observed target, complete panel and final analysis denominators.

Each target used two repetitions of five-fold patient-held-out outer validation, with all visits from a participant assigned to one fold. Training used baseline and week 12 only. Three-fold inner patient validation selected the candidate within each outer training set. Ridge penalties were 0.1, 1, 10 and 100. Continuous targets compared raw-scale and log1p-scale ridge regressions; inverse log1p predictions were not mean-smeared because selection minimized absolute error on the original score scale. Outputs were bounded to 0–10 for PP-NRS and 0–30 for DLQI. Participants had equal total fitting weight, irrespective of their number of development visits. All scaling was fitted within the training partition. The final separate formulas were selected on all baseline/week-12 development records with the same inner procedure.

Recorded IGA was fitted with a penalized proportional-odds ordinal logistic model over the observed categories 0–5. Five thresholds were constrained to increase. The model returns six probabilities, a most probable category, and a secondary expected grade. The primary loss was normalized ranked probability score: the average squared difference between predicted and observed cumulative category indicators over five thresholds. Inner selection minimized participant-average ranked probability score. Ordinal validation also reports exact-category agreement, within-one-category agreement, quadratic-weighted kappa, and probability-based IGA0/1 Brier score and discrimination. MAE of the expected grade is secondary and must not be interpreted as exact-category agreement. The original instrument identity is unresolved, so these are exploratory predictions of the recorded 0–5 grades, not validation of a named vIGA-AD instrument.

BSA-only, three-molecule-only and a training-only constant reference were evaluated on precisely the same patients and splits. The continuous constant was the patient-weighted training median. The ordinal constant used training category frequencies with a 0.25 pseudocount per category. No candidate was selected from week-24 performance. Week 24 represents previously inspected same-cohort temporal transfer.

Metrics were calculated separately for each outer repeat before averaging. We did not average repeat predictions before calculating error. Conditional 95% intervals and paired differences against the reference models used 2,000 patient bootstrap samples of the fixed held-out losses. These intervals do not cover repeated adaptive use of the cohort or assay uncertainty. The ordinal likelihood gradient was checked against numerical finite differences; probabilities were verified nonnegative and summing to one. All exported formulas were checked against the fitted models to numerical precision. Source code, input hashes, selected candidates, split audits and prediction records are retained; the last two contain participant information and are private.

This refit addresses separate targets and current adult eligibility. It does not validate PP-NRS recall-period identity, adult questionnaire identity or the ordinal instrument. The three molecular inputs retain the original positive Summary.xlsx exports. Their assay limitations are documented in the EASI source audit; a dedicated auxiliary-target censor sensitivity has not been performed. The models are research estimates, and the observed errors do not support replacing the patient-reported scores or the recorded ordinal assessment.

Scope of panel choice: the present refit deliberately examines a common, small four-input measurement set. It completes independent fitting and target-appropriate validation under that restriction. It does not exhaust target-specific molecular panels, nonlinear architectures or additional eligible clinical inputs. The historical target-specific panel analyses remain separate supplementary evidence; neither their better numbers nor the present negative common-panel results establish the performance of an untested future panel.
