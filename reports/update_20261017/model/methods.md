# Common-formula model analysis

The October 10 request was evaluated on the same complete-input cohort and patient partitions used for the October 2 frozen model. There were 316 visits from 160 participants: 152 visits at baseline, 89 at week 12 and 75 at week 24. Model development used 241 baseline/week-12 visits from 156 participants. Eligibility required age at least 12 years, an observed EASI, BSA, and all three molecular measurements. No missing clinical outcome was imputed. The existing log2 concentrations were used without changing their assay values. Assay units and cross-platform equivalence remain to be documented from the laboratory source.

All observations from a participant were assigned to the same outer fold. We repeated five-fold patient-held-out validation twice, using the original random seeds. Each outer training set was restricted to baseline and week 12. Within this training set, three-fold patient validation selected the ridge penalty from 0.1, 1, 10 and 100. Each participant had equal total fitting weight, regardless of the number of available development visits. Inner selection minimized the mean of each participant's absolute errors. Centering, scaling and all fitted coefficients were learned from the training fold. Neither current EASI nor a severity category derived from it was used to select the prediction equation.

Three common structures were considered: BSA and three log2 concentrations with an intercept; the same structure with a quadratic BSA term; and BSA multiplied by a linear function of the three log2 concentrations, with no free intercept. The latter enforces an estimate of zero when BSA is zero. It describes a statistical relationship between lesion extent and molecular measurements; it is not a direct measurement of tissue inflammation. The common structures use no treatment, visit-week, phase, age, BMI or previous-score input. A nested selection procedure selected both the common structure and its ridge penalty inside every outer training fold. Its held-out results are the primary estimate of the development procedure's performance. Individual fixed-structure results are exploratory comparisons. The final equation was fitted to all baseline/week-12 development observations after full-development inner selection; this one fixed equation has not received independent validation.

The previous stage-interaction model, BSA-only models with matching structure, and a three-molecule-only model were compared using the same participants and folds. The old stage-specific predictions were reproduced to a maximum absolute difference of 3.6e-15. Treatment-stratified performance excluded ambiguous initial-dose records; these records could remain in common-formula training because no dose label is used there. Leave-one-center-out and leave-one-initial-treatment-group-out analyses excluded all records from the held-out participants before fitting. These are stress tests, not evidence that treatment allocation was randomized.

MAE, RMSE, mean signed error, Spearman correlation, Lin's concordance correlation, R2, calibration intercept and slope, and the proportions within one and two EASI points were calculated separately in each validation repeat before being averaged. MAE was not calculated from the average prediction across repeats. Calibration regressed observed EASI on predicted EASI; ideal intercept and slope are zero and one. Limits of agreement are descriptive mean signed error ±1.96 error standard deviations within each repeat, averaged across repeats. Severity bands were <=7, >7 to <=21, >21 to <=50 and >50. Classification sensitivity and specificity use EASI>7 and must not be equated with every sensitivity/specificity definition in the original p-EASI papers.

The 95% MAE intervals and paired model-error differences used 2,000 patient bootstrap resamples of the fixed held-out errors. They do not include refitting uncertainty, protection against repeated adaptive analysis of this cohort, or assay uncertainty. No interval is reported for a single-patient subgroup. Change estimates were paired within participant, repeat and fold before any averaging, for baseline–week 12, baseline–week 24 and week 12–week 24. Improvement classifications require positive measured and predicted baseline scores; their evaluable denominator is reported explicitly.

Week 24 was excluded from training, parameter selection and final structure selection. It had been inspected repeatedly during earlier work and is reported as same-cohort temporal transfer, not untouched external validation. The very small number of moderate/severe week-24 observations limits the interpretation of low average error.

The rounded published p-EASI coefficients were applied unchanged, with no local refitting, in patients with complete CCL17, IL-22 and IL2RA records. The local IL2RA assay is a nominal proxy for published sIL-2R and lacks a documented cross-platform bridge. Both methods were compared on exactly the same patients at each visit. The published outputs were left unbounded to preserve the original arithmetic; the local estimators were restricted to the EASI range 0–72. This comparison assesses transport of the recorded formula and assay combination, not biological differences between Chinese and Western populations.

PP-NRS, DLQI and IGA were previously fitted independently. Their historical held-out predictions were rechecked against the current clinical values and split files. These outputs are retained as historical exploratory evidence, not renamed as validation of the new common EASI formula. In particular, the older DLQI fitting scope includes 17-year-old participants, whereas the current primary clinical analysis uses age >=18; a separate current >=18 refit is now provided in model/auxiliary_targets and is not substituted into the historical tables. The recorded six-level IGA instrument and PP-NRS recall period still require original source confirmation.

Reproduction: `PYTHONPATH=src python -m cytot2t.curation.unified_model_20261017 --root /path/to/project`. The configuration accepts a project-relative input path. The minimum private input schema is listed in `input_schema.json`. Public calculation: `PYTHONPATH=src python -m cytot2t.curation.unified_bsa_three_calculator example.csv output.csv --input-scale raw --root /path/to/project`. Raw input columns are bsa, CCL17, CCL26 and IL-5. Concentrations must be positive and use the development-assay units. Log2 input mode requires bsa, log2_CCL17, log2_CCL26 and log2_IL-5. Missing or invalid values are rejected. The calculator uses only the Python standard library.

Assay provenance was checked separately for all 948 molecular inputs (316 visits × three markers). Every model input exactly equalled log2 of the positive numeric value in the linked Summary.xlsx export. The analysis did not use the vendor's Filter matrix, replace zero with a pseudocount, use half the observed minimum, or substitute a detection limit. Explicit inequality entries and nonpositive entries had been excluded from the complete-panel input. This does not imply that every positive export was reliably quantified. For IL-5, 9 of 152 baseline, 8 of 89 week-12 and 5 of 75 week-24 exports were below twice the plate-specific software Calc.Low. In a sensitivity analysis, these values were represented by that software boundary, then the entire common-structure selection and ridge tuning were repeated within the same patient partitions. MAE was 3.187, 1.311 and 1.223, compared with 3.194, 1.297 and 1.192 using the original exports. The primary formula and original data were retained. The sensitivity uses a reported software boundary with the documented twofold dilution; it does not establish a validated LLOQ or recover an exact censored concentration.
