# Monitoring display and uncertainty

Use repeat 0 for scatter coordinates and integer confusion matrices. Every point represents one paired patient in one held-out repetition. Changes are later minus earlier; negative values are improvement. Do not connect the three intervals as consecutive steps: they overlap in people and W0→W24 is its own estimand. Observed response uses observed baseline and follow-up EASI; estimated response uses estimated baseline and follow-up EASI. EASI50/75/90 require both baseline denominators to be positive. Counts and excluded denominators are provided per repeat. The primary table evaluates each repetition separately, then averages the metrics. A display of averaged row percentages is also supplied, but it must not be labelled as integer patient counts.

Classification sensitivity, specificity and agreement have separate Wilson 95% intervals per repetition. For each endpoint/window/metric, obtain a two-sided Clopper-Pearson interval at 1-0.05/R in each of R repetitions (97.5% for two repetitions), then average lower limits and upper limits separately. By the Bonferroni union bound, simultaneous coverage of the repeat-specific probabilities implies coverage of their arithmetic mean without assuming independence between repetitions. Pointwise 95% bounds for the arithmetic mean of repeat-specific classification probabilities, under the working assumption of independent Bernoulli patient outcomes within each repeat. Bonferroni controls the repeats within one window/endpoint/metric, not all reported metrics. Repeats may be dependent and are never pooled as independent patients. Conditional assessment of saved held-out predictions; shared training-fit dependence and model-development uncertainty are not propagated, so this is not an exact coverage guarantee for the complete cross-validation procedure.

The old patient-percentile classification intervals are retained only in bootstrap_diagnostic columns. Method references: [SciPy 1.13.1](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.13.1/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.BinomTestResult.proportion_ci.html) and [NIST Bonferroni inequality](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section4/prc473.htm).

For continuous metrics, bootstrap intervals resample patients, carrying all saved predictions for that patient together across repeats. The procedure quantifies conditional held-out prediction uncertainty and does not refit model selection. The ECDF is the average of within-repeat fractions below an absolute-error threshold. Its intervals are pointwise. The published-formula comparison is paired at the patient and visit level; both raw and [0,72]-clipped comparisons are retained. A negative MAE difference favours the current estimator, but BSA access and unresolved assay equivalence mean it is not a fair isolated comparison of algorithms.
