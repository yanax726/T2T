# Methods for the IgE sensitivity analysis

This post hoc analysis followed discordance between available-case and complete-three-visit IgE trajectories. We retained the original 163-patient clinical eligibility rules and initial treatment groups. IgE was recorded at W0, W12 and W24; no W4 IgE was observed, and no W4 value was generated. The primary missing-data sensitivity population was CAMS (130 patients), where all three treatments had measured follow-up IgE. The full 163-patient analysis was additional because Huashan had no dupilumab IgE at either follow-up.

All available within-patient pairs were analysed separately for W0–W12, W0–W24 and W12–W24. Means and paired t intervals used log(1+IgE); exponentiated changes are ratios of 1+IgE, not ratios of arithmetic means. Available-case and fixed-three-visit levels were summarized on this same scale as well as by raw medians. An available-data maximum-likelihood random-intercept model included treatment-by-visit, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre where variable. Treatment-specific baseline means were free rather than constrained equal. The model used all observed primary-visit IgE values and assumed missingness was ignorable conditional on its model; it did not identify missing-not-at-random effects.

We performed fully conditional imputation in patient-wide format with four independent chains. Each chain used 200 burn-in cycles and retained 100 draws spaced 10 cycles apart. The incomplete variables were the three IgE measurements and W12/W24 EASI; complete predictors were initial treatment, age, sex, BMI, baseline EASI and centre where variable. Other available waves and clinical measurements supplied auxiliary information. EASI auxiliary draws were retained only in memory and were never used as clinical validation truth. Two imputation specifications were retained: type-1 predictive mean matching on log(1+IgE), drawing among the five closest measured donors from the same initial treatment and visit; and Bayesian Gaussian regression for natural-log IgE, with residual-variance and coefficient uncertainty. Gaussian IgE draws remained positive and had no imposed upper ceiling. Clinical EASI auxiliaries used predictive mean matching under both specifications. Measured values were fixed throughout. Both approaches assumed common conditional predictor associations across initial groups, with treatment intercepts; the small follow-up sample did not support separate high-dimensional imputation models per group.

We pooled group-specific log-scale means and paired changes using Rubin variance and Barnard–Rubin small-sample degrees of freedom. We additionally fit W12/W24 ANCOVA to each completed dataset with baseline log(1+IgE), treatment-by-visit, baseline EASI, age, sex, BMI and centre where variable; the previously estimated baseline generalized-overlap weights were held fixed, and covariance was clustered by patient before Rubin pooling. These comparisons remain observational associations conditional on the weighting specification. No reduction or switch reassigned the initial group.

Sensitivity analyses multiplied only missing follow-up 1+IgE draws by 0.5 or 2, with a lower physical bound of zero for IgE. Baseline imputations and every measured value were unchanged. These illustrative MNAR scenarios were not estimated from the data. We inspected chain mixing, effective imputations, Monte Carlo error, fraction of missing information, donor support and predictive tails. Repeated recorded values of 5000 were retained without assuming an undocumented assay limit. Thus, neither imputation method was treated as a corrected truth or selected because of the direction of its result.

Interpretive summary: The complete-case discrepancy is explained by changing baseline composition, most clearly in abrocitinib 200 mg at W24. Partially observed visits can be used without requiring all three samples. The W24 abrocitinib conclusion is materially sensitive to the missing-data specification and remains uncertain.
