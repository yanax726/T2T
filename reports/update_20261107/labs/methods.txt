EOS week-4 display estimate. No dupilumab patient has an observed week-4 eosinophil count. For an explicitly defined endpoint-complete group, each patient's week-4 value was estimated by linear interpolation of log(1+EOS) between observed baseline and week 12, followed by back-transformation. The group median and a 95% percentile interval were calculated using 2,000 patient bootstrap samples. The baseline/week-12 group and complete three-visit group were reported separately. These are retrospective interpolation estimates conditional on temporal shape, not observed data or validated individual predictions. Raw-scale linear, early logarithmic-time and delayed quadratic-time interpolation were sensitivity scenarios. None permits an unobserved transient rise, so the scenarios do not span all clinically plausible paths. Masking observed intermediate visits supplied limited interpolation backchecks, not validation of dupilumab week 4. No estimated point enters treatment comparisons or clinical prediction validation.

Baseline molecular table. All 23 markers were summarized in recorded pg/mL directly from positive observed patient-level assay concentrations. Median and quartiles use type-7 linear interpolation on the original scale. No additional dilution multiplier or substitution for censored results was used. Each marker-treatment cell reports its own numeric denominator, with counts outside quantification and other unavailable states retained.

Fixed-patient molecular changes. Within each marker and initial treatment, the same patients had observed positive concentrations at weeks 0, 12 and 24. Baseline log2 concentration was subtracted from each patient's follow-up value, then averaged. Baseline is zero by definition. Pointwise Student-t 95% intervals and two-sided paired sign-flip tests were used only for at least five pairs; sign flips were enumerated for at most 15 patients and otherwise used 49,999 Monte Carlo draws with plus-one correction. Simulated tests with provisional q between 0.04 and 0.06 were refined by exact enumeration when n was at most 24, or 1,999,999 Monte Carlo draws otherwise, before recalculating the same family. This numerical precision check did not change the hypotheses, effects or populations. Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment covered 138 planned marker-treatment-time comparisons (23 x 3 x 2). Non-estimable hypotheses contributed p=1 to that family and have no displayed q value. Stars indicate q<0.05 within a treatment; they do not indicate treatment differences. These new tests use the fixed triplet population and do not reuse q values from the prior two-visit analyses.

Post hoc IEC-compatible EASI-and-itch states. At each of weeks 0, 4, 12 and 24, low disease activity was EASI <=7 AND PP-NRS <=4; very low disease activity was EASI <=3 AND PP-NRS <=1 (IEC consensus, DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2026.0662). Recorded scores were not rounded or imputed. The primary proportion and Wilson 95% confidence interval use only patients with both components observed at that visit. All initial-treatment patients remain in the missingness accounting. A missing component leaves the AND state unknown unless an observed component already exceeds its threshold, which establishes failure. Full-cohort bounds count all unresolved states as failures or successes, while preserving these known failures. Bounds are not confidence intervals. The 24 treatment-specific cells and eight pooled cells are reported without new significance testing. These are single-visit EASI-branch states, not the full AHEAD MDA definition or sustained control.
